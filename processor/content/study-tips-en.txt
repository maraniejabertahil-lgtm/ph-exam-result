5 Tips on How to Study for the Board Examination

Here are the tips in English, organized clearly for review:

1. Use Your Anxiety as Motivation

Do not let anxiety, fear, or pressure stop you from studying. Instead, use these feelings as motivation to work harder and prepare yourself for the examination. Being nervous is normal, but you should turn that nervousness into positive energy.

2. Set Your Goal and Stick With It

Set a clear goal that you want to achieve. Believe that you can pass the board examination and remain committed to that goal. The first step in achieving your goal is believing that you can do it. Do not allow difficulties or distractions to make you lose sight of what you want to accomplish.

3. Have a Work Plan*

Create a study plan and organize your review schedule. Know what subjects you need to study, how much time you should spend on each subject, and what topics you need to prioritize. Follow your plan consistently instead of studying without direction.

4. Find a Study Habit That Works for You

Develop a study routine that matches your learning style and personal habits. Everyone has a different way of learning, so discover the study method that helps you understand and remember information effectively. Be consistent with your routine.

5. Take Your Diagnostic Examinations Seriously

Do not underestimate diagnostic examinations or practice tests. Treat them seriously because they help you evaluate your knowledge and identify your weaknesses. Use the results to determine which subjects and topics require more attention. They also help you become familiar with answering examination questions.

3. HAVE A WORK PLAN*

An Essential Strategy for Preparing for Any Type of Examination

Having a work plan is one of the most important steps in preparing for any examination. Whether you are taking a professional licensure exam, board exam, civil service exam, university entrance exam, academic examination, or certification test, having a clear and organized study plan helps you manage your time, stay focused, and prepare effectively.

Studying without a plan can lead to wasted time, unfinished topics, and unnecessary stress. A good work plan gives you direction and helps you know what to study, when to study, and how to measure your progress.

Here are the important steps in creating an effective study plan:

1. Identify and Organize All Examination Subjects

Start by identifying all the subjects and topics included in your examination. If an official syllabus, examination coverage, table of specifications, or study guide is available, use it as your reference.

Break each subject into smaller and more manageable topics. Instead of writing “Study Science,” list specific topics such as Biology, Chemistry, Physics, and other areas covered by your examination.

This makes your study plan more organized and helps you avoid overlooking important topics.

2. Set Specific and Measurable Study Goals

Every study session should have a clear objective. Avoid setting general goals such as “I will study today.” Instead, identify exactly what you want to accomplish.

For example:

* Understand two specific lessons.
* Read and summarize one chapter.
* Answer 50 practice questions.
* Memorize important terms and definitions.
* Review mistakes from the previous examination.

Setting specific goals allows you to measure your progress and gives you a sense of accomplishment after every study session.

3. Prioritize Your Subjects and Weak Areas

Not all subjects require the same amount of attention. Some topics may be easy for you, while others may require more time and effort.

Identify your strengths and weaknesses. Give more study time to subjects or topics that you find difficult, frequently forget, or consistently get wrong in practice examinations.

However, do not completely neglect the subjects you already understand. Continue reviewing them to maintain your knowledge.

Remember: Spend more time improving your weaknesses while maintaining your strengths.

4. Establish a Consistent Daily Study Schedule

Create a realistic daily schedule that fits your available time and responsibilities.

For example:

Sample Daily Study Schedule

7:00 AM – 8:00 AM: Review previous lessons
8:00 AM – 10:00 AM: Study Subject A
10:00 AM – 10:30 AM: Break
10:30 AM – 12:00 PM: Study Subject B
12:00 PM – 1:00 PM: Lunch and rest
1:00 PM – 3:00 PM: Study Subject C
3:00 PM – 3:30 PM: Break
3:30 PM – 5:00 PM: Answer practice questions
7:00 PM – 8:00 PM: Recall and review the lessons studied

You do not necessarily need to study for 10 to 12 hours every day. What matters most is consistency, concentration, and the quality of your study sessions.

Choose a schedule that you can realistically follow and maintain.

5. Set Deadlines for Completing Each Subject

Your study plan should have deadlines. Do not allow yourself to spend too much time on one subject while leaving other subjects unfinished.

Divide your preparation into four phases:

Phase 1: Foundation Stage
Focus on understanding the basic concepts and covering all the topics included in the examination.

Phase 2: Intensive Review Stage
Revisit important lessons, strengthen your understanding, and concentrate on subjects or topics where you are struggling.

Phase 3: Practice Examination Stage
Answer practice tests, quizzes, and mock examinations. Use the results to identify areas that still need improvement.

Phase 4: Final Preparation Stage
Review important concepts, formulas, definitions, key information, and previously identified weaknesses. Avoid trying to learn everything from scratch at the last minute.

Adjust the duration of each phase depending on how much time you have before your examination.

6. Monitor and Evaluate Your Progress

A study plan is not something you create once and simply follow without checking your progress.

Regularly evaluate your performance by asking yourself:

* Have I completed the topics scheduled for this week?
* Do I understand what I have studied?
* Which subjects are still difficult for me?
* Am I improving in my practice test scores?
* What mistakes do I frequently make?
* Do I need to adjust my study schedule?

Keep track of your completed lessons, practice examination scores, and mistakes. This will help you determine whether your current study strategy is effective.

If you are falling behind or not improving, do not hesitate to adjust your plan.

7. Include Repetition and Retention in Your Study Plan

Studying a topic once does not guarantee that you will remember it during the examination.

Make time for regular review and use effective learning techniques such as:

* Active Recall: Test yourself by trying to remember information without looking at your notes.
* Spaced Repetition: Review previously studied topics at increasing intervals.
* Flashcards: Use question-and-answer cards to remember important terms, concepts, and information.
* Practice Questions: Answer questions related to the subjects you have studied.
* Summarization: Write down the important points using your own words.
* Teaching Method: Explain what you have learned as if you were teaching someone else.

Avoid relying entirely on rereading and highlighting. Make your study sessions active by challenging yourself to remember and apply what you have learned.

SAMPLE 12-WEEK STUDY PLAN

If you have approximately three months to prepare for an examination, you can organize your preparation this way:

Weeks 1–4: Foundation and Coverage

* Organize all subjects and topics.
* Study the basic concepts.
* Complete the initial coverage of the examination syllabus.
* Identify difficult topics.

Weeks 5–7: Intensive Review

* Deepen your understanding of important lessons.
* Focus on weak subjects.
* Review previously studied topics.
* Answer practice questions regularly.

Weeks 8–10: Practice and Evaluation

* Take mock examinations.
* Simulate actual examination conditions.
* Analyze your mistakes.
* Improve your speed, accuracy, and understanding.

Weeks 11–12: Final Preparation

* Review important concepts and information.
* Revisit your weakest areas.
* Practice recalling lessons without looking at your notes.
* Maintain a healthy routine and get enough rest.

You can shorten or extend this plan depending on your examination date and personal circumstances.

IMPORTANT PRINCIPLES TO REMEMBER

An effective study plan should help you:

1. Organize all examination subjects and topics.
2. Set clear and measurable study goals.
3. Prioritize difficult subjects and weaknesses.
4. Maintain a consistent and realistic schedule.
5. Set deadlines for completing lessons.
6. Monitor and evaluate your progress.
7. Practice answering examination questions.
8. Review lessons regularly to improve retention.
9. Adjust your plan when necessary.
10. Complete your preparation with confidence.

FINAL REMINDER

Having a work plan does not simply mean creating a timetable and following it every day. It means having a clear strategy for how you will prepare, improve, and achieve your examination goals.

Remember that the objective is not merely to finish all the topics before examination day. Your real objective is to understand the lessons, retain the information, apply what you have learned, and develop the confidence to answer the examination effectively.

A good study plan gives you direction. Consistency keeps you moving forward. Discipline helps you reach your goal.
